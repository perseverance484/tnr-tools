## 2. Large / medium / small pixel-cluster hierarchy

**Large clusters — composition and volume.** Block the weapon, grip, ring, energy mass and clean negative space into unmistakable shapes. Primary clusters describe the convex blade and twisted wind volumes; broad dark groups visually support the highlights. Form reads without microdetail. Preserve the weapon's recognizable silhouette even under the effects.

**Medium clusters — anatomy and sculptural planes.** Articulate forged facets, bevel transitions, grip wraps, energetic eddies, overlapping curls, turbulences, and shadowed cavities with contiguous shaped pixel groups. Each medium cluster changes apparent surface angle, depth or movement. Respect taper and overlap; use intentionally stepped contours.

**Small clusters — accents with purpose.** A limited number of single-/few-pixel touches indicate edge sparkle, crystalline fracture, specular corners, wind tips, cut marks or chipped material. These sharpen focal hierarchy; they do **not** fill empty areas as noise. Every small cluster must belong to a larger form.

Pixels must remain intentional at native viewing size. Smoothly painted material subsequently reduced or auto-pixelated is not equivalent to authored pixel clusters.

## 3. Sculptural supernatural effects

The effect must feel like a *three-dimensional supernatural material interacting with the object*, not decorative graphics placed behind it:

- Establish an origin and causal connection: chakra emerging from runnels, stressed seams, an edge strike or the material's core.
- Give energy distinct near, mid and far surfaces. Use occlusion, width changes, dark undersides, compression and widening to make spirals twist through space; allow the weapon to cut across the effect.
- Alternate turbulent broad masses with narrow accelerated tips. Design asymmetry, counter-currents, detached wisps and restrained fragments where they strengthen the motion.
- Let negative space breathe; preserve holes between turns, not featureless solid clouds. Use light as a reward on certain edges, not a uniform glow on every band.
- Distinguish effect vocabularies by item: a wind kunai may have twisting spirit-wind; a fire kunai might have tapering tongues and char; earth should carry planes, fracture and particulate weight. V10.1 does not mandate ribbons or a spiral for every element.

## 4. Hue-shifted ramps and material separation

Use deliberately stepped *hue-changing* ramps rather than a single base hue with brightness increments. Choose ramps from each item's materials and approved family palette:

- Polished iron/steel: graphite-blue shadows → cool gunmetal planes → desaturated cyan/silver facets → scarce near-white glints.
- V10 predecessor spirit-wind (observed example, not a claim about V10.1): near-black deep teal/forest troughs → emerald volumes → cool jade/mint surfaces → selective acid-lime/yellow-green luminous edges.
- Wrapped grip: warm near-black leather/wood shadows → rust-brown midtones → sparse copper highlights, contrasting the cold steel and green wind.

These are illustrative examples, not numerical palette requirements or a mandate that all items use predecessor V10 colours. Avoid global flat outlines, uniformly saturated bands, pasted-on bloom and white highlights of equal strength everywhere. Hand-select edge and internal texture pixels by material.

## 5. Material ↔ effect interaction

Wind/elemental light must change nearby weapon surfaces: local green bounce on bevels, thin light in the blade incision, weaker reflected hue at the collar, and dark occlusion where energy passes behind solid metal. Keep the strongest light aligned with the weapon's narrative focal point. Grip, ring, metal and chakra should have visibly different textures and reflectance. The object remains physically constructed, not a featureless silhouette behind effects.

## 6. Readability and assessment hierarchy

| Review scale | Acceptance question |
|---|---|
| **256** (native working review) | Are pixel clusters deliberate, facets sculptural, materials differentiated and energy physically interlocked? |
| **125** (client delivery width per current spec; re-read spec on production) | Does the item read instantly, with one primary focal highlight and a controlled, coherent supernatural effect? |
| **64** (stress) | Does shape/type/element remain legible with secondary details reduced to support? |
| **32** (stress) | Does the distinctive weapon silhouette survive? Is the effect supportive rather than a confusing blur/blob? |

Compose deterministic thumbnail previews with nearest-neighbor scaling and on a dark or checker transparency proof surface; do not use a generative sheet as evidence. Evaluate each individual plus its siblings; thumbnail collisions between siblings are a blocker even when the larger illustration is beautiful. A separate 45px frame check may be used where the existing family brief requires it.

## 7. Identity-preserving overhaul lock

1. **First reference:** the *actual current item* and captured record; lock its name/id, holdable type, distinctive silhouette parts, proportions, orientation, iconic markings and elemental reading. Flag any unverified source as such.
2. **Second reference:** V10.1 or another *approved* quality benchmark, clearly labeled style/material-only; borrow cluster craftsmanship, not anatomy.
3. **Third reference:** current/approved family context. Compare silhouette, rarity and element distinctions, and reserve family relationships.
4. Change a defining silhouette, lore motif or family base **only by an explicit director-approved redesign exception**, recorded per item. Historical proposals (e.g. the 2026-10-25 elemental-kunai family base) remain proposals until the new pilot is accepted.
5. Produce one individually accepted pilot before expanding a wave; each sibling still receives its own visual and export acceptance.

## 8. Explicit failure patterns

Reject **repeated parallel bands** with constant width and shine; **decorative effect ribbons** with no depth, origin or influence on metal; **pillow shading** that simply brightens inward; **fake detail noise** or random isolated pixels; **painterly blur**, airbrush haze or smooth gradients disguised with a pixel filter; and **auto-pixelated smooth art**. Also reject universal equal-bright highlights, uniform neon outlines, flat underlit weapon materials, tangent collisions, and detail that survives only at oversized presentation resolution.
