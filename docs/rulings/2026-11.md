## RUL-2026-11-01-093 — The Seedvault: an uber boss fight opened by a four-shard key

**Date:** 2026-11-01

**Domain:** Skyglass event / new content (design)

**Status:** ACTIVE (design stage). Design of record: the Claude Doc "The Seedvault: Uber Boss Design" (https://claude.ai/artifact/NCAgKDCJSmsjMy6UgiPRZJ); a repository copy is made when the design is final.

**Ruling:**
1. The uber encounter is two new bosses, Sayo, Mother of Echoes and The Silence (a dread entity, not a command), fought solo in three acts (the two keepers together; Sayo's memory game; Sayo, Vessel of the Silence). Inspiration: Uber Shaper and Elder, The Maven. Hard through mechanics, not inflated numbers; both wear AI Heavy Armor and deal high damage.
2. Entry is a straight boss fight, not a Nightmare Crownhold climb: the Seedvault Key, crafted from four tradeable shards (Keel: full Wreck clear 1%; Crown: Crownhold 15%; Tide: rare/tainted chart runs 3%; Ledger: Haul Reserve chests 5%), each also craftable from 100 Salvage; the key is untradeable and spent at the start.
3. Players with the stun/seal-immune Ancient Tailed Demon bloodline are a tuning case, not the baseline.

**Evidence:** Director, 2026-11-01: "I want something akin to Uber Shaper and Elder, or even the Maven from PoE"; "I recommend equipping AI Heavy Armor and giving them high amounts of damage, not moderate"; "2 new unique well named and themed bosses"; "The Silence is a better name"; "I like The Silence more as a dread entity"; "this should just be a straight boss fight done with a rare key"; "Yep, that format is approved."

**Canonical destination:** the design doc above; later `docs/design/SKYGLASS_SEEDVAULT.md`.

## RUL-2026-11-01-094 — The uber fight's canon: Sayo, the Stilling, the Mother Glass

**Date:** 2026-11-01

**Domain:** Skyglass event / story (canon)

**Status:** ACTIVE. Amends RUL-2026-11-01-093 (names and lore). The design doc and `docs/design/SKYGLASS_STORY_BIBLE.md` are updated from this.

**Ruling:**
1. All skyglass grows from **the Mother Glass** beneath Crownhold, found and grown by **Sayo, Mother of Echoes** (the Project's Glasswright). Its roots woke **the Silence**, an ancient stillness in the deep mountain that hungers for stillness and cannot act without an instrument.
2. Under its influence Sayo performed **the Stilling**: on the last night of the siege she drew the entire army of Crownhold (soldiers, officers, the ship's crew, the Crowned Captain) into the Mother Glass. Crownhold stopped answering.
3. Every afterimage in the event is one Sayo **casts** from a stilled impression, fixed at the instant of the Stilling (hence the unending siege order). A broken afterimage returns **thinner**; the Silence drinks the **remainder**.
4. Sayo is the event's mastermind: she called the ship home, used the charts, the Lantern and Crownhold to measure and lure strong shinobi, and needed the player to break the Crown (the Crowned Captain's afterimage was the one will contesting hers). The four shards are her invitation. She wants to still the player: "You will make for a good impression."
5. Names: the fight **The Mother of Echoes**; Acts **The Glasswright and the Deep**, **The Stilling**, **A Good Impression**; merged form **Sayo, Saint of Stillness**; movements **The Quiet**, **Every Name in the Glass**, **The Long Hush**; enrages **Unbridled** / **Lucid**; the key **An Invitation in Glass**; Witnesses **the Worn**; safe tiles **Where the Three Refused**. Naming follows four voices (Glasswright, Silence, Crownhold, the Stilling) with the glossary in the design doc. "Seedvault", "Root Lattice" and "UNTIL" are retired.

**Evidence:** Director, 2026-11-01, lore session: "make it like this, the Great Recall ... sucked them all into the Root Lattice ... The Silence is ... a separate entity drawn out of its slumber deep in the mountain ... She is not holding it back so much as succumbing to it"; "Sayo is essentially a mad genius ... the mastermind behind the entire event"; "Sayo has been luring the player. 'You will make for a good impression.'"; "Looks really good."

**Canonical destination:** `docs/design/SKYGLASS_STORY_BIBLE.md` (new final chapter) and the design doc.

## RUL-2026-11-02-097 — The Mother of Echoes: tuning directions after the first simulation

**Date:** 2026-11-02

**Domain:** Skyglass event / uber encounter balance

**Status:** ACTIVE. Directs tuning pass 2 on `fable/mother-of-echoes-sim` (pass 1 head 79b4d307: skilled full clear 26.7% against a 10-20% target; the average model dies in round 2; immunity changes nothing; Silence-first 33.3%).

**Ruling:**
1. **Act II threat:** the Silence's Hush pools deal heavy damage that grows over time. Act II is no longer a pure race against the enrage. The band is reached by mechanics, not by choosing damage reduction 79 or 80.
2. **Stillness becomes a telegraphed stun,** so stun immunity is a real counter. Act II Sayo keeps stunprevent, so a stun can never cause an unmarked blast.
3. **Average players:** recalibrate the average gear model first. Lower the Glass Lancet opener only if they still can't reach Act II. Target: under 5% full clear, and they reach Act II.
4. **Lucid is strengthened** so the Silence-first and Sayo-first orders clear at about the same rate.
5. **Healing denial uses healing reduction** (director, 2026-11-02, amended the same morning): `decreaseheal` at 100% (theninjarpg@3314d122 tags.ts decreaseHealGiven) cuts heal, lifesteal, vamp and absorb on the consequence whatever their source, gear lifesteal included, which healprevent misses (P3). It goes on the Hush floor pools or a method ALL cast, whichever survives Clear and cleanse. It is blocked by debuffprevent. Siphon (a shield conversion) is not covered; pass 2 measures whether it matters.

**Evidence:** Director, 2026-11-02, answering the five questions from pass 1: "The silences pools can deal heavy damage and grow over time"; Stillness as a stun; "Recalibrate, then soften"; "Strengthen Lucid"; "You can use healing reduction to prevent lifesteal. Either a floor effect or method all".

**Canonical destination:** `harness/skyglass_wreck_sim/mother_of_echoes_spec.json` and the design doc's Tuning section once pass 2 lands.

## RUL-2026-11-02-101 — Telegraph marks move off `visual`; live Omen marks fixed; Etching readable for one round

**Date:** 2026-11-02

**Domain:** Skyglass event / combat presentation (live library marks and the uber)

**Status:** ACTIVE.

**Finding (reproduced offline at theninjarpg@3314d122):** after every action, `server/api/routers/combat.ts:857-859` keeps only the `visual` ground effects created in or after that action's round. A `visual` telegraph therefore disappears at the first action of the round after its cast, whatever its `rounds` value, while the eruption still lands. The director saw this live: "when I move off the tile the omen marked the effect instantly disappears". Probe: scratchpad `omen_probe/`.

**Ruling:**
1. **Telegraph art goes on a non-visual ground carrier:** `increaseregeneration`, power 1, rounds 1, friendlyFire FRIENDLY, carrying the mark's art. The `visual` is removed. Lingering-pool art goes on the lasting effect itself (friendlyFire ENEMIES).
   - **Accepted cost:** a battle-log line on each cast ("marked the ground with increaseregeneration").
2. **The live Skyglass Omen marks and Eruption pools are fixed now,** through an unequip, edit and re-equip set (push/430+). It is run Monday to Thursday.
3. **The uber's Etching marks are readable for one full round** through the carrier, not flashed. The memory challenge comes from the delay before the recast.
4. **Harness:** the uber harness models the visual filter. Earlier dodge results assumed marks that live players can't see.

**Evidence:** Director, 2026-11-02: "1. Yes 2. Readable for 1 round".

**Canonical destination:** `docs/design/SKYGLASS_BOSS_ANIMATIONS.md`. Its renderer description is out of date: the pin uses the pixi renderer, `libs/rendering/scenes/combatEffects.ts`, not three.js.

## RUL-2026-11-05-116 — Event structure framework: Skyglass is the reference; diegetic progression

**Date:** 2026-11-05

**Domain:** content design / all events

**Status:** ACTIVE. The principles are the director's; the framework's checks, templates and anti-patterns are Claude's drafting, open to revision. Its open questions (§8: standard pacing targets, minimum routes, the progress surface, crafting gates on sinks, starved nodes, stated tier labels, Skyglass follow-ups) are not settled by this entry.

**Ruling:**
1. **Future events are structured on the Skyglass model:** interlocking content that feeds each other; an economy of sources and sinks; progression and depth; player agency (more than one route to an outcome, X → Y and Y → X, free to change approach); and legibility (the player can see what they did, what is ahead and behind, and trace their progress).
2. **Diegetic progression:** every event has an explicit progression ladder for designers (tiers from entry to finale, with prerequisites and gates) that players never see as numbers. Each rung is expressed in the world: an item that is the prerequisite, places that read as deeper, and enemies, dialog and descriptions that signal the tier. Designers fix the start and the end first, then fill the rungs with story and worldbuilding.
3. **The framework is recorded** in `docs/design/EVENT_STRUCTURE.md`, with the Skyglass event mapped as the reference in `docs/design/SKYGLASS_EVENT_MAP.md`.

**Evidence:** Director, 2026-11-05, verbatim:
- "I kind of see this event as the holy grail of content development for tnr. Not because it is perfect, but because it is the flagship that features how I think content should be structured. It should feed into each other and create an economy of sorts where doing this part of an event feeds into that one, unlocks this or that, makes this possible. It creates a sense of progression and depth for the player, allowing them to engage with content with agency. Being free to choose their approach and pursue their desired outcome and being able to adjust that approach at will is what defines a good game to me. Knowing they can do X thing to get Y outcome or Y to get X and so on. Being able to explore a game and know what you did, what's in front and behind you, trace your progress, it's important. We should look into recording a cohesive event structure future development can draw on."
- "It used to be that games just labeled things Level 1, Level 2 and it was obvious what your progression was and what the prerequisites were. Now a good game to me tells you that kind of information by weaving it into the story rather than telling it to the players face. You are at Level 1 of the Skyglass Wreck because you've just got a chart that leads to some buried chest in the sand, and Level 100 because you've got 4 fragments that unlock a hidden area that leads to the Final Boss Sayo who blows up the floor and shoots death everywhere and has an immunity phase from her shadow and yadda yadda. We tell a story, paint a picture, but the underlying framework is there. We can say where we start and end and then fill in the gaps with the story and worldbuilding."

**Canonical destination:** `docs/design/EVENT_STRUCTURE.md` (routed from `docs/00_INDEX.md` task routing: event design).
