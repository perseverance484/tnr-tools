# The Unwritten War - Mission-by-Mission Storyboard

[Companion to 28_ARC v2. Narrative beats only; numbers and rewards remain placeholders. Beats marked (obj) are engine objectives; everything else is dialog.]

## Ch1-4. The Absorbed Openers (procedural vignettes, one village)

**Ch1. Training Grounds** [live]: the tutorial mission; in arc hindsight, the first lesson is literally "trust your instructor's version of events."
**Ch2. The First Mark** [live]: a doctored record surfaces in a routine job; nobody cares. First taste of the method.
**Ch3. The Bell That Lied** [live, ours]: a false bell empties a district; storehouses are robbed on schedule. The player catches the thief: a hired cutout who was paid through a dead-drop and knows a method, not a face. The arc's thesis in miniature: the signal lied, and everyone obeyed the signal.
**Ch4. The Courier Who Never Arrived** [FIRST BUILD]: a route is intercepted so cleanly the village logs say the courier never existed. The player walks the route backward, finds the courier's cache, and delivers what the record says was never sent. Closing beat: the recipient checks the ledger and pauses: "then who signed for it?"

## Ch5. The Day the Bells Agreed (hook; all villages; investigation, no combat)

1. Cold open: five one-line vignettes, one per village register: a monument re-carved, a shrine ledger rebound, a treaty stone reset, a print plate re-set, a relay schedule renumbered. Nobody notices. That is the point.
2. The Archivist's introduction: a cramped room of duplicate records kept out of paranoid habit. Her line sets the arc: "The world and my copies disagree. One of them has been edited, and I bind my own books."
3. (obj) Collect: the mismatched ledger page from the public hall, while the clerk insists it has always read this way.
4. (obj) Move: the defaced monument; the new carving is weathered EXACTLY like the old stone. Teaching reset if the player calls it fresh work: she corrects you like a misfiled page.
5. (obj) Collect: a stone rubbing made by a dead scholar decades ago; it matches her duplicates, not the monument.
6. (obj) Deliver both proofs to her. She lays them side by side. CLUE: five villages in one hour is not a thief, it is an institution.
7. HOOK OUT: her duplicates are now the only dissenting record in Seichi. "Which makes me," she says, dry as paper, "a filing error. They will come to correct me."

## Ch6. The Paper Garden (Shirohana; HEIST)

1. Shirohana by night: the village that prints Seichi speaks in margins and impressions. The presses never sleep; that is the problem.
2. The press-master (future red herring) swears his plates are true. The Archivist's test page proves the master plates were swapped years ago: Shirohana has been printing corrections in good faith.
3. (obj) Gather/craft counters: forge replacement press parts quietly, framed as "work that leaves no requisition trail."
4. (obj) Collect: the original plates, hidden in the one place no printer looks, the misprint morgue.
5. Correctors arrive to burn the morgue: calm, apologetic, administrative. (obj) defeat_opponents. Their leader's line: "Nothing is being destroyed. It is being brought into agreement."
6. (obj) Deliver plates to the hidden true-press; the first honest page in years comes off the drum.
7. CLUE: the Hand owns infrastructure, not burglars; it prints, it does not steal. HOOK OUT: the true plates carry a sky-date that exists in no current calendar. "Ask Tsukimori," the Archivist says. "The sky keeps its own ledger."

## Ch7. Watchers of the Waning (Tsukimori; NIGHT THRILLER)

1. Tsukimori speaks in watches and phases. The night-watch logged the sky on the night of the change; stone can be re-carved, the sky cannot.
2. The watch-elder (future red herring: she profits from the corrections) grants access grudgingly, post by post, in the open dark.
3. (obj) Move to star-post one under ambush odds; Correctors hunt the paths between. The walking is the fear.
4. (obj) Star-post two: the ambushers carry village-issue gear. CLUE: the Hand has people inside at least one village office.
5. (obj) Star-post three, then (obj) win an encounter at the shrine sector: take one alive enough to read his orders.
6. The three logs triangulate the missing sky-date: a winter that has been filed out of every calendar but the stars'.
7. HOOK OUT: one man is old enough to have walked that winter, a lantern keeper on a Hyorin pass. "Living memory," the Archivist says, "is the one book they cannot rebind." Beat of silence. "Which means they will not try to rebind him."

## Ch8. The Second Lantern Count (Hyorin; TRAGEDY; the scripted failure)

1. Hyorin speaks in weather and distance. The pass road is the old mission's route; returning players walk their own memory.
2. The keeper greets the player BY NAME, warm, unhurried: proof of what is at stake in a single line of dialog.
3. (obj) Route-walk the lanterns while he talks: Glacier-era testimony, the winter that was filed away, spoken plain by someone who lived it.
4. Corrector signs on the route; the pace turns. (obj) Defeat the cell-leader in the high pass: a real, winnable, WON fight.
5. Return to the hut. The keeper is alive, unharmed, mid-chore. He greets the player as a customer. Politely. As a stranger. The fight was the diversion; the correction already happened.
6. (obj) Collect his original route ledger from the store shed, hidden years ago and never filed. He watches you take it with friendly incomprehension.
7. CLUE: the Hand does not kill what it can rewrite; in its doctrine, erasure is mercy. Closing node holds victory and loss in one breath: every lantern lit, and the man who taught the route gone while still standing there. HOOK OUT: the unfiled survived. So would anything never finished.

## Ch9. The Unforged Name (Akasumi; CHARACTER STUDY on an escort)

1. Akasumi speaks in heat and hammer-rhythm. The founders' blade was never finished, never named, never filed: the smiths guard it like a grudge, not a treasure.
2. Gate dialog, then (obj) the arc's one ambush-opener start_battle at the forge doors: the Hand arrives BEFORE negotiations, a first.
3. The guild-mother explains why the blade is safe: "You cannot edit a sentence that was never written down. We stopped writing on purpose."
4. (obj) Collect the unforged blade; (obj) carry it across two sectors, the weight of it narrated at each leg.
5. Intercept: a Corrector cell-leader offers to BUY it first, at triple value, paperwork ready. CLUE: violence is the Hand's failure state; ledgers are its weapon of choice. Refusal, then (obj) defeat_opponents.
6. (obj) Deliver to the Archivist's courier at the wind-road post.
7. HOOK OUT: the courier's report: every road east is watched, every relay logged. "Then we stop trickling," the Archivist writes back, "and run everything at once."

## Ch10. Letters on the Wind (Akikaze; RACE)

1. Akikaze speaks in wind and schedules. All five proofs move in one night by relay, and the Hand owns the roads.
2. (obj) Leg one: collect from the drop, deliver through a watched pass; (obj) fights between hand-offs.
3. Teaching reset on a wrong hand-off: the relay-mistress redirects you like a mis-sent parcel, in schedule-clerk deadpan.
4. (obj) Leg two: intercepted Corrector orders change hands mid-fight. CLUE: every order is countersigned by an OFFICE seal, and every signature is a different handwriting.
5. (obj) Leg three lands the proofs at the Archivist's vault as dawn comes up the wind-road.
6. For one full chapter, the player has simply won. The Archivist allows herself one adjective. HOOK OUT: the Hand's fortress archive is located; the war goes to its shelves.

## Ch11. The Hall of Five Seals (convergence; GAUNTLET battlepyramid)

1. The archive-fortress: five floors, one per village's stolen memory, each floor gate written in that village's register (print, phases, weather, hammer-rhythm, schedules).
2-6. (obj) Dialog-gated fights per floor, Corrector bands as grunts, the five cell-leaders re-met as floor keepers with continuity lines from their village chapters.
7. Floor archives read between fights. CLUE: the office is centuries old; its holders die like anyone; the pen is passed. Succession records, no founder's name.
8. The vault at the top: (obj) final keeper, then the door. The vault is EMPTY. HOOK OUT: word arrives that the Archivist's vault at home is empty too. Everything delivered in Ch10 is gone.

## Ch11.5. The Corrected World (FALSE VICTORY INVERTED; combat-light)

1. Return to villages that now remember wrong: the recovered records re-corrected IN PLACE, weathered like the monument was. Two village offices post notices praising the Hand's "restorations."
2. (obj) Dialog revisits: the press-master recants, the watch-elder endorses the corrections, and the Archivist stands publicly discredited as the forger of her own duplicates.
3. Confrontation branches with teaching resets: arguing harder makes it worse; the corrected world defends itself, politely.
4. (obj) Collect the one proof that survived: the unforged blade, never filed, sitting exactly where it was delivered, unedited because it is unwritable.
5. The Hand's motive, in its own circulated writing. CLUE stated plainly: a curated memory is peace; the legacy age died of too much remembering; the Hand is finishing a mercy.
6. HOOK OUT: the blade cannot be argued with, only presented. The Archivist, disgraced and precise: "Then we stop arguing with the record. We summon the recorder. A trial needs the office to attend in person."

## Ch12. The Unwritten Hand (finale; TRIAL then boss)

1. The trial: five proof-branches, one per village, each re-established live through its witnesses (the guild-mother, the relay-mistress, the watchers, the print morgue, and the keeper's unfiled ledger read aloud FOR him). Wrong approaches reset with teaching text.
2. The chief revealed per ruling [recommendation: the Office itself: the seat outlived its holders, the current chief only the latest hand on a centuries-old pen; the succession archive and the many-hands countersignatures were the fair clues].
3. The chief's defense is Ch11.5's doctrine, spoken to the player's face, calm as filing.
4. (obj) Dialog-gated start_battle: the boss fight.
5. Branching epilogue per the legacy-mapping ruling: the true history read aloud in five registers; the legacy names honored as history; the Archivist's duplicates become the public record and she becomes its first Keeper of the Open Record.
6. Payoff line of the arc, hers: "A village is what it remembers. Starting today, it remembers that it was lied to, and survived the truth."
7. Rewards [BALANCE]: completion line, memento item, badge candidate. Side content unlocks: the Empty Seat, Five Restorations, the Vanguard crossover.
