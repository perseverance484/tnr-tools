# The Tenth Name - simple version

Three drafts in a row got more complicated, not less. The problem is upstream of the drafts:
the premise itself - a list of names to be decoded - is a detective story. No amount of
trimming turns a decoding puzzle into a mission-of-the-week.

So the premise goes. What stays is the title and one image: **nine names, and a tenth.**

## The rule I should have been designing to

A mission-of-the-week episode is:

1. Somebody wants something, said out loud in the first minute.
2. You go there.
3. One person stands in the way, and they have a reason you can understand.
4. One fight.
5. One truth that changes what the job meant, delivered in a line, not a deduction.
6. You go home slightly worse off.

Twelve to fourteen nodes. One dead end, for flavour. No pattern to assemble, no offices, no
schedule. If a player needs to hold three facts in their head at once, it is the wrong quest.

---

## Option 1 - The stonecutter's list (quieter)

**The want.** A stonecutter has nine names on a memorial and room for a tenth. A squad was
lost on a border job eleven years ago. Nine are carved. The tenth man was recorded a deserter,
so his name was never cut, and his daughter has been paying the stonecutter every season for
eleven years to hold the space.

She hires you: find one piece of proof he did not run.

**The obstacle.** In a village two days out, somebody is living under the dead man's name -
using his registration, his seal, his pension. He has been doing it for eleven years, which is
exactly how long the daughter has been paying.

**The fight.** He is not a monster. He is an old shinobi with a stolen identity and everything
to lose, and he fights because there is nothing else left to try.

**The truth.** The dead man did not desert. The desertion notice was filed by the man wearing
his name, because a deserter is not looked for. He has the tenth man's kit in a box, including
the thing that proves it.

**The cost.** The proof clears the name and destroys a living man's whole life. You carry both
back. The stonecutter cuts the tenth name; the daughter does not thank you, because she has
been paying for eleven years to be told this.

**Nodes: 12.** One dead end - the pension office, where an afternoon establishes that a dead
man has been collecting money and nobody has ever considered that strange.

---

## Option 2 - Ninth crossed off (louder)

**The want.** A bounty sheet with ten names. Nine are crossed off. A hunter has been working
down the list for two years and is one name from finished. The officer wants the tenth name
brought in **alive and first**, because the tenth is a seventeen year old forger who has been
on that sheet since he was twelve, for a seal his master pressed.

**The obstacle.** The hunter. Faster than you, ahead of you, and entirely within his rights -
the bounty is legal, the sheet is real, and he has nine receipts.

**The fight.** You reach the boy first by half a street. The hunter is not interested in
arguing about whether a twelve year old forges anything.

**The truth.** The boy knows every name on that sheet. He wrote most of them - it was his
master's list, and the master sold it to the hunter and then put his own apprentice on it to
close the circle.

**The cost.** Bring the boy in and he is safe and finished, a name in a village ledger forever.
Let him run and the hunter simply keeps hunting, and you have made a two year problem into a
lifelong one. Neither is rescue.

**Nodes: 13.** One dead end - the master's old shop, burned out and picked over, where you
learn nothing except that somebody got there years before you.

---

## Which

Option 2 is closer to the shape asked for - a chase, a rival with a legitimate claim, one
fight, a truth in one line, a choice that costs. Option 1 is the better story and the slower
one.

Either can carry the title. Neither needs an investigation.

## What this means for the existing record

Both are a rebuild of The Tenth Name, not a pass. `push/35` is discarded on either.
`push/33` (Copies) and `push/34` (Old Ghost) are untouched by this and still ready to run.

If either lands well, **Copies, Not Thefts should get the same read** - it is the other quest
in the set built around a document reveal rather than a person.
