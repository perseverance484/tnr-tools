# Skyglass skyship - engineering proposal v1

**Status:** PROPOSED, 2026-10-01. User requests engineering and physical design before more production art (RUL-2026-10-01-008). Dimensions, energy source, hardware, operating limits and the precise accident below are design proposals, not approved TNR canon or game mechanics.
**Baseline:** live main verified at `df17803a9c4222025dba7e68c1919a372835fcd6`; owned branch `chatgpt/ghost-ship-skyglass-art-20261001`.
**Scope:** original fictional engineering, ship arrangement and future art guidance. The magical premises are explicit; this is not a physically validated aircraft construction plan. Preserve the 57-node quest, five endings, existing roster slots, shared combat kits and reward contract.

## 1. Design purpose and architecture

A covert military transport and mobile command vessel carries a small elite team over difficult terrain, approaches under concealment, deploys operatives, and retrieves them. Expensive specialists and a unique power plant explain the medical and emergency-crew systems. Give it restrained protection around machinery and personnel, practical boarding gear and repair access; its mission does not require an ornamental galleon or a broadside battery.

Use the approved two-mast timber/iron identity as the starting envelope. The latest exterior is a mood study, not an engineered drawing. New dimensions, Core construction and internal arrangement are proposals; do not force incidental generated details into the design.

The architecture separates four things:

1. **Energy:** light collected by the sails, and chakra charge supplied at a military berth or from prepared canisters.
2. **Skyglass:** a conditioned crystal that converts, stores and regulates that energy into a current the ship's seals can use.
3. **Techniques:** engraved arrays turn the current into suspension, wind thrust, concealment, medical support and temporary copies.
4. **Control:** physical valves, shutters, linked seal plates and crew-operated helm instruments govern those arrays. The Crown supplies command authorization and precise synchronization.

Skyglass is neither an unlimited fuel source nor a thinking entity. Crew impressions provide identity and control patterns, not free energy.

## 2. Ship arrangement

**Provisional envelope:** 28 m hull length, 7 m beam, about 4 m hull depth amidships, two unequal battened collector rigs. The taller forward mast rises about 11 m above the main deck. Use 40-60 tonnes as an early loaded-mass sizing envelope, not a calculated displacement or approved payload rating.

| Position | Function and construction |
|---|---|
| Stern / raised helm | Forward visibility, protected steering station, accessible wind-thrust controls. Chart room and crew berths below. |
| Midships | Core well between the mast foundations; weight carried into keel, floor beams and transverse frames. Ward and galley beside the access route. |
| Forward lower hull | Secured supplies and armaments, with the existing vault/strongroom. Heavy stores stay low and near the center; they cannot be piled on the bow without retrimming. |
| Main deck | Uncluttered movement around the protected Core hood; rail-height boarding gate, folding gangplank, mooring lines and descent ropes. Moor or hold station for boarding. |
| Hull underside | Three pairs of lift-seal plates; a small central crystal relay supervises trim. The exposed keel crystal is a relay, not a second primary engine. |
| Bow and rig | Bow crystal, mast tips and rail studs define the cloak boundary. The existing bow housing has a clear function. |
| Aft hull | Two recessed wind-array grilles under the quarterdeck; access from inside, clear of boarding stations. |

Timber carries longitudinal and transverse loads, with iron straps reinforcing joints and guarding seal channels. Insulated conductive strands run beside structural ropes: tugging a stay must not stress a crystal connection. Core supports, mast steps and lift plates share strong frames. An ordinary sea hull supported only at one glowing keel point would impose the wrong load path.

The lower deck has a working passage around the Core and between existing rooms. The arrangement sheet locates equipment rather than specifying every partition. Add mundane needs at the detail stage: stairs/hatches, drainage, heads, ventilation, water, spare sail panels and repair lockers. Living crew originally still needed food, air and sleep; vital support reduced exhaustion and stabilized casualties, not ordinary biological needs.

## 3. What Skyglass physically is

**One continuous crystal body:** a tall, flattened, six-sided spindle, approximately 2.4 m high, 0.65 m across its broad face and 0.40 m deep. It has long planar faces, faceted shoulders and tapered ends. This gives it a consistent front, side and top profile that future art can reproduce.

At rest it is translucent smoky blue-gray. Fine milky growth layers and a darker central inclusion are visible inside it. Under controlled load, those layers brighten pale white-blue in a slow, ordered pulse. Heavy output increases internal brightness; a fault produces competing pulses and a branching fracture. It is not a permanent white lamp or a spray of unrelated crystal spikes.

The Core means the crystal **plus its apparatus**:

- **A fixed cradle, about 3.2 m tall:** blackened iron uprights on a stone-lined foundation, with padded saddles and ceramic isolation. The crystal is supported, not floating or carrying hull loads.
- **Three regulating collars:** upper collector input; middle balanced supply to the ship; lower working reserve and recovery. Segmented conductive surfaces and replaceable inscribed plates let engineers regulate flow without carving deep stress notches into the crystal itself.
- **A protected reference seal:** a living shinobi originally conditions the crystal to a stable chakra pattern. That impressed pattern can persist without continuing biological contact.
- **Crew register plates:** eight named specialist impressions stored in sealed ceramic plates around the lower cradle. This follows the current named roster; it is not a new total-complement rule. Crowned Captain uses the Captain's record; Charge and Canister are hardware.
- **Protective shutters:** iron-faced sliding or hinged panels close around the upper assembly. The maintenance cage seen in the art is not sufficient combat protection by itself.
- **Cooling and discharge paths:** a stone-lined well, passive air passages and a controlled discharge path handle conversion losses. These do not make overload harmless; blocked vents and prolonged heavy demand remain problems.

The upper assembly sits within the familiar central deck housing, while the lower assembly is accessible from the lower hull. Its placement and protective detail must be approved before altering the locked icon. The diagram opens the shutters to show construction; operational art need not show the entire naked crystal.

## 4. Energy source, charge and instability

**Proposed special property:** after being conditioned by a shinobi's chakra, Skyglass can convert harvested light into a stable potential that inscribed techniques can draw as chakra. This is a new property specific to the project. It is not presented as established TNR energy physics, nor as an ability every crystal possesses.

The battened sails carry a woven collecting seal. Energy gathered over their area travels through insulated rigging to the upper collar. Skyglass accumulates charge; the reference seal keeps its output compatible with the ship's arrays. Prepared canisters and a berth connection permit charging before a night operation. Living specialists can supply a costly emergency top-up. A summoned copy cannot recharge the Core for a net gain.

The collector rigs earn their size. More exposed area helps charging, but creates wind load and signature. Reefing is necessary in dangerous winds and reduces collection. Clouds slow charging; night provides no useful solar replenishment. Stored capacity and safe output are separate: a full crystal can still fracture if too much is demanded too quickly.

In conceptual terms:

`change in reserve = usable collected light + supplied charge - lift - propulsion - cloak - crew systems - losses`

Use consistent fictional energy units during future sizing. Do not invent a precise speed, endurance or wattage without specifying collector area, conversion efficiency, storage capacity and seal demand together. The current target is that daylight collection sustains economical operation, while concealment, hard maneuvering and multiple copies draw down the reserve. Charged capacity and continuous output are design limits, not quest timers.

Long-term operation also requires a positive daily energy balance: useful collection must cover the whole day's demand and losses. The cloak/recharge cycle does not itself guarantee this. In poor weather the vessel must reduce activity, settle at a holding site or suspend nonessential echoes; it cannot continue full operation indefinitely on an empty reserve.

Skyglass becomes unstable when charge passes too rapidly through imperfect or damaged growth layers. Heating, strain and uneven output reinforce each other. The regulating collars limit this; a physical crack makes regulation unreliable. This is a material and control failure, not nuclear fission or supernatural evil awakening inside a gem.

## 5. Lift, propulsion and control

**Lift is the explicit fantasy assumption:** a sustained suspension technique applies upward force through the six reinforced hull zones. It does not erase mass or inertia. The crystal alone does not levitate an attached ship without that array.

Real load accounting still helps: a hypothetical 50-tonne loaded vessel requires roughly 490 kN of total support just to hover. This illustrates the scale of the structural problem; it does not establish that our proposed seal plates have been validated to provide it. The supports must distribute that load and maintain balance around the center of gravity.

- Equal increases across the lift zones raise the ship; equal reductions lower it.
- Fore-aft differences control pitch; port-starboard differences control roll.
- Two stern wind arrays accelerate surrounding air aft for propulsion. Unequal output turns the ship, and movable discharge vanes allow braking and low-speed reversal.
- Sailcloth can stabilize the rig and use favorable winds, but is principally a collector. Do not assume a freely floating vessel can tack like a waterborne sailing ship without a lateral reaction force.
- Pendulum trim indicators, a mechanical helm linkage and marked control levers make the ship operable by trained personnel. The Crown improves synchronized command; it does not remove the need for navigation, trim, engineering or deck work.

A split supply network and an isolated pair of reserve canisters preserve controlled descent after an ordinary main-feed failure, provided the lift array itself is intact. They cannot replace destroyed structural lift zones or provide endless emergency power. Guards protect machinery because it has physical limits.

## 6. Cloaking: the recommended simplification

**Prefer optical/chakra concealment to literal disappearance from reality.** This revises the preceding unapproved refuge-space proposal. The ship remains physical and follows normal time. Its sudden apparent materialization is its cloak dropping.

The bow prism aligns a concealment field whose edge is carried around the entire ship by mast-tip and rail relays. It diverts the visible light that would reveal the hull and reduces outward chakra leakage. This is a sealing technique powered by the Core, distinct from its ability to store energy.

Boundaries make it credible:

- Light diverted around the vessel also misses the collectors. Full cloak prevents meaningful solar recharge.
- It hides neither mass nor displaced air, wake, sound or collisions. Rain, smoke and dust can reveal its outline; a strong sensor may still detect leakage.
- Small observation apertures and passive directional sensing permit cautious navigation, at the cost of local imperfections. High speed and sudden maneuvers strain concealment; this is a covert approach mode, not invisible unrestricted combat.
- Deployment ropes and a boarding gate cross the boundary. Opening them requires a local break or lowering the cloak.
- Low reserve drops concealment before it sacrifices essential lift. A more coherent Captain cannot repeal that energy limit.

This explains apparent ghostliness with the equipment the ship was built to have. If true intangibility remains desired, it requires a separate, explicitly approved technique and rules for energy intake, collisions and boarding; it is not assumed in this proposal.

## 7. Emergency copies and the curse

Each original elite operative was registered through a bodily preservation seal. The system monitored their condition, supplied limited restorative support, and retained a chakra impression. If an operative could not serve, a temporary copy could take over a registered duty while the original recovered. This is the initial military purpose of the emergency-crew function.

The Core supplies energy; register plates supply identity and trained habits; station seals form the physical chakra body. Copies can handle the ship and use their established combat skills. Their number and duration compete with all other demands. Existing multiple-Warden encounters can draw on the same registered pattern without introducing an unlimited army.

Recommended fate remains that the originals died in the accident. The damaged preservation binding continues to interpret their absent living signatures as an emergency requiring substitutes. The curse is its compulsory persistence: it keeps calling the same crew back to duty. No new trapped-soul or biological-survivor premise is needed.

During an appearance the echoes have continuous awareness and can act. Stable identity remains in the register; the damaged return path retains later experiences poorly. They remember duty and training better than intervening incarnations. A brief uncertain remark about elapsed time supplies the intended tragedy. No personal journal subplot is required.

The Captain has the strongest command impression. The Crowned Captain is its clearest synchronized manifestation, not a second original person or an all-knowing observer of every year. Crew remain complete, with restrained aura and afterimages. The shore Sentinel uses the existing cache's linked guard seal and local stored charge; do not generalize that exception into freely roaming copies.

## 8. One accident, one continuing operating failure

1. On the return from a mission, an attack damages a regulating collar and its isolation. The crew demand escape thrust, concealment and casualty support together.
2. The unstable crystal fractures under the load. A surge reaches the linked preservation bindings before isolation completes; the living crew are lost. This outcome remains a proposal.
3. The emergency register calls substitutes and the concealment system engages. Observers see a violent discharge and the vessel disappear, and conclude it was destroyed. The sponsors suppress the failed military project.
4. The physical ship survives with a damaged Core. Its echoes maintain the surviving machinery, follow remembered routes and perform their assigned duties.
5. Its concealment order keeps drawing charge while starving collection. The lift safeguard eventually drops the cloak; the ship becomes visible, collects light and recalls whichever crew duties it can sustain. As reserve recovers, concealment engages again.

This is an operating cycle, not a time loop. Appearance length depends on charge, light, weather, load and damage; no exact interval is imposed on quest gameplay. If it emerges at night it must remain exposed or settle at a holding site until it can recharge. It cannot solve this by sending copies to recharge their own source.

Years of survival require ordinary upkeep as well as power. Copies can use the vessel's real tools and supplies, patch sails, tend joints and operate pumps; timber and metal are not regenerated from chakra. Show weathering, patched collectors, sealed-off damage and dwindling spares. Its low-demand holding periods and old remote routes explain long absence from public notice more economically than claiming unlimited continuous combat flight.

## 9. Consequences for the existing art and quest

The Warden guards boarding and machinery access; Oathkeeper controls registrations and binding seals; Wayfinder handles navigation and concealment alignment; Arsenal Keeper maintains physical stores; First Blade protects command; the engineer's unstable impression is Core Horror. Keep their approved short names and existing encounter functions. Physical rewards stay physical, including Skyglass Serum and the Crown/Fragment.

For ship art, retain the dark timber/iron identity, unequal collector sails and broad hull silhouette. Replace arbitrary extra glowing crystals with specified relays or equipment. Use one primary spindle in its protective housing. Rigging lights indicate routed energy; hull light follows actual lift zones. Persistent stern fragments in the earlier icon can read as faulty optical afterimages, not lost physical hull pieces floating free.

**Production gate:** review this engineering proposal before generating a new Core or ship asset. Then make one controlled Core study from the physical specification. Revise the ship reference if approved changes require it, and only then resume scene production. The whole-echo Deck Warden remains the next character prototype. No existing approved pixels, quest wiring, combat kits or implementation branches were changed by this design pass.
