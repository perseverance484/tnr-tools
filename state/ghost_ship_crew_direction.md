## Premise and correction

The vessel was a top-secret military project staffed by elite shinobi specialists. Every human aboard when it vanished was a high-level operative, including the personnel responsible for navigation, stores, engineering and security. Their short titles identify project duties, not low military standing.

The cursed-ship feeling must be immediately visible. The approved explanation already supplies the curse: an emergency preservation lattice endlessly reconstructs the crew because their mission never reached an end. Duty has outlived life. These are stored chakra impressions with surviving personalities, not conventional undead.

## Shared visual grammar

- Elite status must survive removal of the blue accent: specialized armor architecture, fitted near-black layers, protected seal instruments, controlled posture and purpose-built weapons. Avoid an ordinary field uniform with glowing scratches added afterward.
- Share one family of blank faceplates, overlapping iron seal plates, dark lacquer, wrapped conductive cords and pale white-blue edges. Use the locked ship's actual rib, crystal and seal geometry for continuity. No village/clan insignia or modern tactical equipment.
- Give each lesser reconstruction one major, readable failure: a missing section, empty armor, displaced outline or altered face. The body must visibly fail to resolve; extra rips in clothing alone do not communicate this. Retain human eyes, expression or habitual behavior so the tragedy stays personal.
- Use hard-edged missing areas, opaque afterimage fragments and restrained edge light. Preserve the TNR spec's dark palette and pixel discipline; no spectral fog, bloom, full-body neon, photographic decay or gore. The higher-fidelity command pair becomes more coherent rather than more damaged.
- The approved ship remains the scene reference. Its intact dark structure and impossible operation can frame the tragedy without changing the locked hull or sail plan. Interior clues can include empty duty stations, untouched rations and repeatedly maintained seal fittings.
