## Logline

Someone is rewriting what Seichi remembers, village by village, and by the time anyone notices, the old truth will have no witnesses left. Five villages, five stolen memories, one hand holding the pen.

## Theme

**A village is what it remembers.** The Hand's project: erase the legacy age so the current one can be rewritten around whoever holds the record. The arc runs on METHOD then SCOPE then IDENTITY, and its escalation is personal cost: first the player loses nothing, then time, then a witness, then a victory they thought they had banked.

## Writing style guide (binding for all chapter dialog)

- Dialog nodes are 2-4 sentences, mobile-read length; one image per node maximum: a sound, a texture, a wrongness. No lore dumps; lore arrives as testimony, complaint, or grief.
- Each village chapter carries its village's register in the narration: Shirohana speaks in print and margins, Tsukimori in watches and phases, Hyorin in weather and distance, Akasumi in heat and hammer-rhythm, Akikaze in wind and schedules.
- Choice buttons are characterization: short, kinetic, in the player's voice, never "Continue" where a stance is possible. Wrong-path resets teach in-world (the Archivist corrects you the way she corrects a misfiled page).
- The Correctors never rage; they amend. Their menace is administrative calm. The Archivist is dry, precise, allergic to adjectives; the lantern keeper is warm and unhurried; give every recurring NPC one verbal tic and keep it.
- No em dashes in any dialog description or choice text. Chakra/ninja terms only.

## The chain (13 chapters; each prerequisite on the last, questType story, new builds hidden)

Chapters carry a GENRE REGISTER and a CLUE (one true fact about the Hand) so the middle never repeats itself and the reveal plays fair.

**Ch1-4, the absorbed openers** (Training Grounds [live], The First Mark [live], The Bell That Lied [live, ours], The Courier Who Never Arrived [designed, FIRST BUILD]). Genre: procedural vignettes. Clues: the method exists (records, signals, routes); the hands are hired cutouts who know nothing. In arc framing these were field tests on one village.

**Ch5. The Day the Bells Agreed** (~L20, hook; investigation, no combat). One founding record in each of the five villages becomes wrong at the same hour, and only the Archivist's private duplicates disagree. Palette: dialog branches with teaching resets, collect_item x2, move_to_location, deliver_item. CLUE: scope (five villages in one hour means an organization, not a thief). HOOK OUT: her duplicates are now the only dissenting copies in Seichi, which makes her the next target.

**Ch6. The Paper Garden** (Shirohana, ~L30; genre: HEIST, played for tension not tragedy). The village that prints Seichi's records has been printing corrections for years unknowingly; the player runs a night operation to pull the true plates before the morning print run. Palette: gathering/crafting counters, collect press parts, deliver to the hidden true-press, one defeat_opponents vs Correctors burning the morgue-files. CLUE: resources (the Hand owns infrastructure, not burglars; it prints, it does not steal). HOOK OUT: the recovered plates reference a sky-date that does not exist in any current calendar.

**Ch7. Watchers of the Waning** (Tsukimori, ~L40; genre: NIGHT THRILLER). The watchers logged the sky on the night of the change, and the sky cannot be rebound. Palette: move_to_location x3 star-posts under heavy attackers percentages, win_encounter_at_location at the shrine sector, dialogs between legs. CLUE: reach (the ambushers carry village-issue gear: the Hand has people inside at least one village office). HOOK OUT: the star log names the missing date, and the only man old enough to remember that winter keeps lanterns on a Hyorin pass.

**Ch8. The Second Lantern Count** (Hyorin, ~L50; genre: TRAGEDY, and the arc's SCRIPTED FAILURE). The lantern keeper is living memory, so the Hand comes to correct him. The player fights through and WINS every fight, and still arrives at a rewritten man: the keeper alive, unharmed, and remembering a different life, greeting the player as a stranger. Mechanical truth preserved (no fake loss; the failure is narrative). Palette: route-walk of the old mission's lanterns, collect his hidden original ledger, defeat a Corrector cell-leader, closing dialog where victory and loss share a node. CLUE: motive glimpsed (the Hand does not kill witnesses when it can rewrite them; erasure is mercy in its doctrine). HOOK OUT: his hidden ledger survived because it was never filed; the unfiled and unfinished cannot be corrected.

**Ch9. The Unforged Name** (Akasumi, ~L60; genre: CHARACTER STUDY on an escort frame). The founders' blade was never finished and never named, so it cannot be edited; the smiths guard it like a grudge. The arc's one generic start_battle, as a dialog-gated ambush opener. Palette: opener battle at the forge doors, collect the unforged blade, carry across two sectors (move_to_location), defeat_opponents intercept, deliver to the Archivist's courier. CLUE: doctrine confirmed (a Corrector cell-leader offers to BUY the blade first; the Hand prefers ledgers to bodies, violence is its failure state). HOOK OUT: the courier network reports every road watched; the last relay must run everything at once.

**Ch10. Letters on the Wind** (Akikaze, ~L70; genre: RACE). All five proofs move by relay and the Hand owns the roads. Palette: three collect-then-deliver legs across mixed sectors, defeat_opponents between legs, one reset-teaching branch on a wrong hand-off. CLUE: identity shape (intercepted Corrector orders are countersigned by an OFFICE seal, not a person's name; every signature is a different hand). HOOK OUT: the relay lands the proofs, and for one chapter the player has won.

**Ch11. The Hall of Five Seals** (~L80; genre: GAUNTLET; story battlepyramid, Tower doctrine, single completion, full payout at top [BALANCE]). The archive-fortress, five floors, one per village's stolen memory, floor gates written in each village's register, keepers are the five cell-leaders. CLUE: the fortress archive holds the Hand's own history: the office is centuries old and its holders die like anyone; the pen is passed. HOOK OUT: the top floor's vault is already empty. The proofs the player delivered in Ch10 are gone from the Archivist's vault too.

**Ch11.5. The Corrected World** (~L85; genre: FALSE VICTORY INVERTED, the counterstroke; short, combat-light). New chapter per analysis. The player returns to villages that now remember WRONG: the recovered records were re-corrected in place, the Archivist stands publicly discredited as a forger, and two village offices post notices praising the Hand's "restorations." Palette: dialog-heavy revisits (one per two villages plus the Archivist), collect_item (the one proof that survived: the unforged blade, never filed), reset-teaching confrontation branches. CLUE: motive stated by the enemy in writing: the Hand believes a curated memory is peace, that the legacy age died of too much remembering. HOOK OUT: the blade cannot be argued with; the finale is a trial the Hand must attend in person.

**Ch12. The Unwritten Hand** (~L90-100; finale: TRIAL then boss). Confrontation through heavy branching dialog, five proofs re-established live (each village's testimony a branch), wrong approaches reset with teaching text, one dialog-gated start_battle vs the chief, branching epilogue canonizing the legacy age per ruling. **Chief identity: analysis recommends option (c), the Office itself: the seat outlived its holders and the current chief is merely the latest hand holding a centuries-old pen: strongest fit for the theme (a village is what it remembers; an office that remembers nothing but its function), fairest to the seeded clues (Ch10's countersignatures, Ch11's succession archive), and it leaves a lawful sequel hook (an office can be abolished and still be re-founded). DECISION RESERVED.** Payoff: true history read aloud, legacy names honored as history, the Archivist's duplicates become the public record, memento item + badge candidate [BALANCE].

## Side-content slate (standalone, prerequisite-gated; design after their anchor chapter ships)

1. **The Margin Notes** (prereq Ch5; Shirohana, dialog/collect): the Archivist's past: whose records she failed to keep once, and why she duplicates everything now. Character study, no combat.
2. **Red Ink** (prereq Ch6; small 2-quest chain): a Corrector defector wants out; the Hand does not fire employees, it re-files them. Escort + safehouse dialog maze. Deepens the Correctors from uniform menace to people.
3. **The Sky That Stayed** (prereq Ch7; Tsukimori, counters + dialog): help the watchers re-derive the erased winter from star math: gathering/training counters as "observation shifts." Quiet epilogue chapter for the thriller.
4. **A Lantern for a Stranger** (prereq Ch8; Hyorin, route-walk, no combat): tend the rewritten keeper's route beside him; he does not remember you, and the quest never fixes it. The arc's grief valve. Ends with him teaching the player a route he technically no longer knows.
5. **The Apprentice's Name** (prereq Ch9; Akasumi, craft counters + defeat): a young smith wants to finish the unfinishable blade; the guild forbids it for exactly the reason the arc proved.
6. **Dead Letters** (prereq Ch10; Akikaze, 3-leg relay, comic register): the relay's backlog of ordinary mail from the war week: a love letter, a bill, a cat complaint (crosslink to the Magistrate's cat). Palate cleanser that humanizes the stakes.
7. **ANBU Docket: Corrector Cells** (prereq Ch11; repeatable bounty in the Wayward Contracts pattern): mop-up cells with three archetype targets. Mechanical bridge from story to repeatables.
8. **The Vanguard Reads the Record** (prereq Ch12 + Tower access; crossover, dialog-heavy): the nameless girl from the Tower asks the Archivist to find who she was before the sisters chose her; the honest answer is a blank page and what she does with it. Links the two candidate mythologies without merging them.
9. **The Empty Seat** (prereq Ch12; short chain, sequel hook): the abolished office's regalia must be archived or destroyed; villages disagree. Seeds any future season.
10. **Five Restorations** (prereq Ch12; one per village, tiny errands): hanging the corrected records publicly; five 5-minute epilogues, one per village register.

## NPC throughline plan

The Archivist: appears Ch5 onward every chapter; arc from paranoid clerk to publicly disgraced (11.5) to first Keeper of the Open Record (12). The lantern keeper: Ch8 pivot, side 4 epilogue; never restored, by design. Corrector cell-leaders: introduced in their village chapters, re-met in Ch11 with continuity lines. The defector (side 2) can cameo as a Ch12 trial witness if built by then. Red herrings for the chief: the discredited Shirohana press-master (too obvious), a Tsukimori elder who benefits from the corrections (motive without means).
